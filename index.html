IX. Parallel Turning

Parallel turning reduces a rotating workpiece to a cylinder of uniform diameter by feeding the tool parallel to the lathe axis (carriage/saddle feed).

9.1 Setting up a cutting tool

  • Clamp the tool in the tool post with the shortest possible overhang for rigidity — less chatter.
  • Set the tool tip at center height (check against a tailstock dead center or a steel rule on the workpiece face).
  • Tool above center: clearance is lost and the heel rubs. Tool below center: the work rides over the tool and leaves a pip when facing.
  • Set the tool square to the work (slight lead angle) so it cuts on the side-cutting edge.

9.3 Kinds of shoulder

  • Square shoulder — 90° to the axis, made with the side-cutting edge.
  • Filleted shoulder — corner radius for strength.
  • Chamfered (angled) shoulder — beveled, commonly 45°.
  • Undercut (recessed) shoulder — relief groove so mating parts seat fully.

X. Drilling, Boring, Reaming and Tapping

10.1 Spot and drill work in a chuck

  • Face the end, then spot (center) drill so the twist drill will not wander.
  • Hold the drill in a drill chuck or taper shank in the tailstock; the work is gripped in the 3-jaw chuck.
  • General-purpose drill point angle: 118°. Larger drills → lower rpm.
  • Peck the drill (withdraw) to clear chips and let coolant in.

10.2 Boring, reaming and tapping

  • Boring: enlarging/truing a drilled hole with a single-point boring bar — keep the bar overhang short.
  • Reaming: finishes a hole to precise size and smooth finish; leave ≈0.1–0.25 mm, run at ~½ drilling speed, never reverse while cutting.
  • Tapping: cuts internal threads. Tap drill ≈ major diameter − pitch (M10×1.5 → 8.5 mm). Sequence: taper, second, plug (bottoming) tap. Use low speed and cutting fluid.
